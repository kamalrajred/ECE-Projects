# Business and Cost Analysis

[← Back to project README](../README.md)

Beyond the prototype, the course asked for a value proposition, stakeholder analysis, and cost analysis. That part of the work showed that the prototype was designed with a real use case in mind.

## Value proposition

The dual-axis tracker advances autonomous marine technology and the wider adoption of solar-powered vehicles. It is a more cost-effective and energy-efficient alternative to gas-powered boats for remote data-collection work.

## Stakeholders

| Group | Who | Why they care |
| :-- | :-- | :-- |
| **Primary** | Marine research institutions, environmental monitoring agencies | Marine-life tracking, water-quality monitoring, hydrographic mapping |
| **Secondary** | Defense contractors, offshore energy companies, private academic research institutions | Data collection in remote areas that are only safely accessible by autonomous vehicles |

### Two product options

1. **Fully integrated.** The tracker is built into a new autonomous boat, for the best hardware and software integration and maximum efficiency.
2. **Standalone attachable system.** It bolts onto existing gas-powered vessels. This lowers the barrier for stakeholders who are reluctant to buy a whole new boat, and gives them a reason to come back after more development.

## Cost analysis (full-scale unit)

Costs are split into capital expenditures (hardware to build and deploy a unit) and operating expenditures (maintenance and operation). The capital estimate covers the five technical systems needed for the boat to operate as designed, plus data handling.

| Technical system | Estimated cost |
| :-- | :-- |
| Dual-axis gimbal | $20,000 to $30,000 |
| Marine-grade solar panels | $1,500 |
| Marine-grade battery | $1,500 to $2,500 |
| Hull (full integration) | $5,000 |
| Propulsion | $800 |
| Data processing | $100 to $200 |
| Data collection | $500 to $10,000 |
| **Total per unit** | **about $29,400 to $50,000** |

> The report's text quotes an upper bound of about $45,500, but the line items in its cost table add up to $50,000. This page uses the sum of the table. The lower bound of $29,400 matches the table in both.

Data processing and collection costs vary widely with the type of data being gathered, so they are given as estimates.

## Recommendation

The report recommends investing, for three reasons:

1. **Modular design.** Components attach and detach without time-intensive disassembly, which cuts maintenance time and labor cost, and frequent maintenance and repair are a fact of life for boats.
2. **Better energy efficiency** than boats with fixed panels. This matters most on a moving vehicle in a disruptive environment, where a fixed panel is often not facing the sun. More energy in means electronics powered longer and better boat performance.
3. **Adaptability.** The dual-axis tracker is not limited to boats. It could serve autonomous land vehicles, unmanned aerial vehicles, and stationary objects such as buoys.

### Risks acknowledged

- Navigation in harsh climates, where severe conditions could damage the boat.
- Logistics of when and why the boat returns to its base station, needed to minimize risk.

The report judges these to be solvable with further investment.

## What this demonstrates

Thinking past the circuit to the customer: who benefits, what it costs, what the risks are, and how the product could be packaged.
